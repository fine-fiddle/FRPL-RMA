A higher or lower residual means a higher or lower assessment result than the model predicts. BEHIND THE COMPARISON

A clearer comparison.
A still-limited model.

The difference between where a school should land, and where it does land, is exciting and illuminating. But it’s foolish to believe the school is responsible for the entire difference. Parents, families, neighborhood culture, and how screens are warping their heads matters more. A teacher can make a difference! But let’s not kid ourselves.

Can states, regions and tests be compared?

Comparison population: Chicago Public Schools. Select Statewide for Illinois IAR and SAT comparisons.

The selected state’s assessment standards apply. Each state sets its own assessment system and proficiency thresholds. The same percentage proficient in two states can represent different standards. Test changes within a state also break direct comparisons. Studentizing residuals does not turn these results into a common achievement scale.

Every model is tied to its source population, year, assessment, level and subject. A region filter and the population used for the regression are distinct choices; the current comparison population is named at the start of this section. Low-income measures retain their source definitions, which may differ between states and reporting systems.

The Illinois pilot imports the 2024 Report Card's separate IAR and SAT rates. Statewide IAR rankings use tested counts published by Education Data Center v3.1 from ISBE records. Grade counts must be exact, cover all expected tested grades, and reconcile to the Report Card rate within 0.11 percentage points. Missing, suppressed or inconsistent counts are excluded. SAT residual rankings are available without sampling intervals because verified tested counts are unavailable. Statewide grade-school history includes 2017 PARCC and 2023–2024 IAR; SAT history covers 2017–2019 and 2021–2024. The 2017 archive supplies school-wide proficiency directly; those residuals have no sampling intervals because verified tested counts are unavailable. Each year uses its own income data and regression. Earlier IAR school aggregates remain unavailable: grade percentages cannot be averaged without valid weights. The 2023 outcome is aggregated from ISBE grade-level proficiency with verified EDC tested counts; rounding differs slightly from the 2024 published school rate. The Chicago view continues to use its matched CPS sources and annual models.

What do NYC school types mean?

School types describe verified programs a school offers, not one exclusive category. A school with both Zoned and Gifted & Talented programs appears under both filters. Results and residuals describe the assessment cohort, not the individual admissions program. Filtering does not refit the regression.

Classifications come from the public MySchools directories for kindergarten, middle school, high school and upper-grade G&T, retrieved September 26, 2026. The source labels its school records 2025–26. These are a dated snapshot of program offerings, not historical admissions rules or a guarantee of current eligibility. The older 2021 directory is not used. Charter status comes from the official school organization directory.

  • Zoned: a program with a published zone, zone priority or zone guarantee. This does not mean every program at the school is zoned.
  • Unzoned / Open: a program explicitly described as Non-Zoned or Open. Residency, sibling and other priorities may still apply.
  • Gifted & Talented: a published district or citywide G&T program; eligibility and priorities vary.
  • Screened: a program that uses academic screening or an additional assessment.
  • Specialized High School: a school identified by MySchools as specialized, including the eight testing schools and LaGuardia.
  • Audition / Arts: a verified audition admissions program, including LaGuardia. An arts-themed name alone is not enough.
  • Charter: charter management verified by school ID. The grade-school assessment source excludes charters; gathered NYSED charter data has not yet been integrated into these rankings. An empty result does not mean NYC has no charters.
  • Other / Special program: a verified method outside the categories above, including educational option, language criteria, talent tests, transfer and specialized service programs. This is not a measure of academic quality.
  • Unclassified: no supported classification was found. It does not imply open or neighborhood admission.

A mixed-grade school may match a type offered in a different entry grade. Coverage is incomplete, and a known label does not establish that every program has been captured. Consult MySchools for the specific program and entry year. Classification source and provenance.

What does the Wisconsin comparison measure?

Grade schools use the Forward Exam in English language arts and mathematics, grades 3–8. The outcome is the share of students with valid scores reaching the top two performance levels, aggregated across the school’s enrolled grades 3–8 with exact counts. School records cover Full Academic Year: students enrolled in the same school since the beginning of the school year. DPI reports school-level Forward and ACT results for FAY students. students, while DPI’s published dashboard percentage uses all enrolled students, including non-testers. These tested-only values can therefore differ from the dashboard. Dynamic Learning Maps, Wisconsin’s alternate assessment for students with the most significant cognitive disabilities, about 1% of students. It is excluded from these models. is excluded. A school enters the model only with complete, non-redacted results for every enrolled grade 3–8; DPI redaction of small grades therefore excludes some schools, and every exclusion is retained in the history file.

High schools use the state-mandated grade 11 ACT with writing: ACT ELA and ACT Mathematics, again the top two levels among students with scores. Exam takers are not a fixed graduating cohort, and the ACT ELA score’s construction changed in 2016-17. DLM grade-11 records are excluded.

Performance levels changed in 2023-24. Before that year, proficiency was Advanced + Proficient; from 2023-24 it is Advanced + Meeting. The two eras are separate assessment identities on this site, and the history line breaks at 2023-24. Forward 2019-20 is absent because the administration was waived; ACT 2019-20 records exist.

Income is the same school year’s Wisconsin Economically Disadvantaged enrollment: direct certification, National School Lunch Program free/reduced-price eligibility (at or below 185% of the federal poverty guidelines), or an alternate household income form. DPI evaluates each student every year, including Community Eligibility Provision schools. Suppressed values remain unavailable and are never imputed. School-wide income and tested populations differ. Wisconsin sources, coverage and limitations

Sources are the public WISEdash data files: Forward Exam archives (2015-16 through 2024-25, with 2019-20 absent), ACT Statewide archives (2014-15 through 2024-25), and the annual third-Friday-of-September enrollment snapshot used for same-year economic status. Directory names and coordinates come from the DPI public schools GIS layer (2026-27, current September 4, 2026), so locations can be newer than the assessment snapshot and schools closed before 2026-27 may be list-only. The state outline is US Census TIGERweb.

Model outcomes are tested-student percentages, not DPI’s enrolled-student dashboard percentages. School results are FAY; the income snapshot is all enrolled students. Performance levels changed in 2023-24, and the two eras are fitted separately. DLM is excluded, and a complete non-redacted grade set is required for grade-school membership, so DPI redaction of small grades excludes a substantial number of schools; each exclusion and reason is published in the Wisconsin history file. The ACT Statewide layout labels its year as expected graduation, but the files are aligned to the test administration year (verified against ACT Graduates).
