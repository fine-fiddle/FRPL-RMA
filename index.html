BEHIND THE COMPARISON

A clearer comparison.
A limited model.

Student populations provide context. They do not set a ceiling on what students can achieve.

What are we comparing?

Spring 2024 proficiency: IAR grades 3–8 for grade schools; SAT grade 11 state standards for high schools. “Reading” uses IAR English Language Arts or SAT evidence-based reading and writing. Combined is the equally weighted mean of the two proficiency percentages, not the percentage proficient in both.

The horizontal axis is CPS low-income enrollment divided by total enrollment in its 2023–24 profile. It is an FRPL proxy, not a verified lunch-eligibility percentage. Demographics describe the entire school, while assessments cover tested grades.

How is the residual calculated?

For each level and subject, ordinary least squares fits proficiency = α + β × low-income percentage + ε. Each eligible school has equal weight. A school’s raw residual is actual minus predicted proficiency, in percentage points.

The externally studentized residual is tᵢ = eᵢ / (s₍₋ᵢ₎ √(1 − hᵢᵢ)), using a residual variance estimate that excludes that school and adjusts for leverage. Zero is the fitted prediction; positive values are above it. The straight-line model may predict outside 0–100%; predictions are not clipped.

Name and school-type filters change what you see, not the regression. Models always use all eligible schools of the chosen level. Grade and high school residuals are never ranked together. Program labels follow CPS’s school-level classification; a school can offer multiple admissions programs.

How do school size and uncertainty enter?

Tested-student counts drive approximate binomial sampling intervals, propagated through the fitted regression and expressed on the studentized scale. Smaller tested groups generally have wider intervals. Rates of 0% and 100% use Jeffreys smoothing to avoid zero estimated uncertainty.

Intervals hold the studentizing denominator fixed. They are conditional sampling intervals, not full confidence intervals for school effectiveness; they omit cohort, demographic, model-choice and student-dependence uncertainty. Combined uses the conservative maximum positive covariance because student-level subject overlap is unavailable.

No multilevel or empirical-Bayes shrinkage is applied in this version. Studentization adjusts leverage; it does not itself adjust for enrollment. Avoid treating small differences or overlapping intervals as definitive rankings.

Sources, coverage and limitations

Suppressed, missing or invalid rates, missing demographics, and tested counts under 10 are excluded from fitting and ranking. Schools without eligible data remain in the directory. Pre-K-only / other school levels are excluded. This is a historical snapshot, not a current admissions directory.
