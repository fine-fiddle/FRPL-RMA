# Michigan 2024–25 source and definition guide

The official 2024–25 files support separate **M-STEP grades 3–7** and **PSAT 8/9 grade 8** populations, matched to same-year school-level individual economic disadvantage. The two assessments have different proficiency standards and must not be pooled into one grade-school response. MI-Access has alternate achievement standards and is a separate assessment population. The release uses native valid-score grade weights and preserves all response suppression. Coverage and validation are recorded below.

The request's delivery address and temporary download links are not source identifiers and are not stored in the repository. The stable public form, selected year/file names and full native-file checksums identify the source.

## Individual income eligibility under universal meals and CEP

The [October 24, 2024 MDE At-Risk funding memorandum](https://www.michigan.gov/mde/-/media/Project/Websites/mde/Memos/2024/10/At-Risk-Section-31a-Funding.pdf?rev=2427d9e20ff54d15b83adfe1660fe04e) is contemporaneous evidence for the 2024–25 reporting definition. It requires districts to review fall economic-disadvantage/Supplemental Nutrition Eligibility (SNE) counts, identifies direct-certification matches as automatically eligible, and requires Education Benefits forms at CEP schools or Education and Nutrition Benefits applications elsewhere to establish additional individual eligibility despite Michigan School Meals. Universal meal access is therefore not the economic-disadvantage numerator. The memo also describes potential decreases after Medicaid renewal flexibilities ended in May 2024; a one-year release must retain that administrative eligibility context.

The [2024–25 Section 31a FAQ](https://www.michigan.gov/mde/-/media/Project/Websites/mde/OES/Programs/Section-31a/Section_31a_FAQ.pdf?hash=E4B6DA7763F511D87C8736882E3B1F58&rev=9cc53929961542f580dda0fa917f7d49), pages 6–7, expressly states that CEP does not make every pupil economically disadvantaged. Household economic information is required for students who are not directly certified or covered by household extension, and is reported through fall SNE. The same FAQ distinguishes eligibility counts from meal reimbursement claims. Section 31a funding uses the previous October's count; **funding allocations are not a substitute for the selected year's school enrollment data**.

The [August 26, 2024 direct-certification FAQ for 2024–25](https://www.michigan.gov/mde/-/media/Project/Websites/mde/ohns/School-Nutrition-Programs/Direct-Certification/2024-2025--Direct-Certification/FAQ-for-DC-Requirements-SY-24-25.pdf?hash=014F75E3EBA43302752F9560978D2777&rev=1f692a3c3e5c411ea77d58624d96568c) defines enrollment matches to SNAP, TANF, Medicaid and foster records. SNE reports individual free/reduced-price meal or milk eligibility and supports the ED population; it is required for each eligible student. Household extensions are permitted for SNAP, TANF and qualifying Medicaid, while foster eligibility is individual. This documentary definition does not authorize reconstructing protected aggregate cells.

The [MI School Data subgroup definition](https://www.mischooldata.org/student-subgroups/) includes approved household applications, SNAP/TANF, homelessness, migration, foster care and certain Medicaid eligibility. The visible income label should identify **Michigan MSDS individual economic disadvantage**, not describe it as a universal-meal percentage, CEP claiming percentage, a direct-certification-only share or an identical national household-income measure. Application nonresponse and administrative eligibility reporting can limit the observed count.

The [Student Enrollment Counts report](https://www.mischooldata.org/student-enrollment-counts-report/) defines an unduplicated headcount on the legislated fall count day, the first Wednesday in October. It is not FTE. Pre-K is excluded from school records after 2012–13, although included in some higher-level aggregates. The native same-record ratio is:

```text
individual economic disadvantage =
    ECONOMIC_DISADVANTAGED_ENROLLMENT / TOTAL_ENROLLMENT
```

Both values must be independently published exact integers, with a positive denominator and numerator between zero and enrollment. Native `<10`, `*`, blanks and other bounds remain unavailable; no complement, midpoint, test-subgroup count or previous year fills them. The income population is the fall enrolled school population, not the spring tested subset.

## Valid-score response, native aggregate and suppression

The [official grades 3–8 report metadata](https://www.mischooldata.org/grades-3-8-state-testing-includes-psat-data-proficiency/) affirmatively defines the reports as **all valid public-school tests**, excludes nonpublic/home-school scores, and defines M-STEP/PSAT proficiency as Advanced or Proficient. `NumberAssessed` is the source's valid-score count in this report population. Enrollment, participation and expected federal 95% denominators must not replace it. The report uses the building where a test was taken, rather than the student's enrollment building; joining exact school identity and year does not assert identical tested and enrolled student populations.

The delivered assessment CSV independently contains `TotalMet`, `TotalDidNotMeet`, `NumberAssessed`, four performance-level counts and `PercentMet`. In visible M-STEP and PSAT records, `TotalMet` is the published at-or-above-proficient aggregate. Native reconciliation must verify `TotalMet = TotalAdvanced + TotalProficient` and `NumberAssessed = TotalMet + TotalDidNotMeet` wherever the independent operands are visible; the four level counts provide a further denominator check. An eligible response uses exact native `TotalMet / NumberAssessed`, checked against the rounded display percentage. A masked individual level is not reconstructed merely because its aggregate is available. An independently visible aggregate can support a response without recovering its protected components. A masked aggregate or denominator cannot be replaced by inferred values.

The [assessment disclosure-avoidance rules](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/cepi/MISchoolData/Grades_38_and_High_School_Assessments_Disclosure_Avoidance.pdf), last updated September 25, 2018 and still linked from the current report, suppress complete rows below 10 assessed students, protect small performance-level counts and their complements, and cap percentage displays according to group size. The native CSV masks remain authoritative. Exact zero is usable only when independently published, never when derived from an unavailable cell. `PercentMet` may be bounded or rounded and is retained for audit rather than used to manufacture an exact numerator. The conservative release guard also requires an exact unsuppressed native `PercentMet`: a protected response percentage is not recreated from visible count pairs. This is distinct from leaving an individual performance-level bin protected while using its separately published, unsuppressed aggregate.

The file has grade records rather than an all-grade school total. Any grades 3–7 aggregation must include the complete eligible native grade set for the school and subject, use verified score counts, reject incomplete or protected required grades, and avoid averaging percentages. Native all-student rows must be selected explicitly; demographic rows are not additional model members. Combined remains the equally weighted mean of the final Math and ELA rates, not pooled subject counts or the fraction proficient in both. Sampling intervals require valid counts for every member of each model.

## Assessment standards and school identity

The report metadata documents M-STEP Math/ELA testing in grades 3–7 and the grade 8 transition to PSAT in 2018–19. The [Michigan grade 8 PSAT performance-level definition](https://www.michigan.gov/mde/-/media/Project/Websites/mde/OEAA/PSAT/PSAT-8_9-for-Grade-8-Performance-Level-Information.pdf?hash=5262B1C6A190B3DEE23168F8015F123B&rev=ae6c6e11dc744013bd37b586f30c2b16) defines proficiency thresholds of 390 for Reading and Writing and 430 for Math; Proficient and Advanced meet the grade 8 College Board benchmarks. This does not make PSAT proficiency interchangeable with grade-specific M-STEP standards. The Spring 2024 SAT-suite digital design transition must be explicit before extending PSAT history. The documented 2025 Social Studies standards reset does not justify labeling Math/ELA as having that reset. MI-Access FI/SI/P use alternate content expectations and remain outside a regular-assessment population unless separately modeled and labeled.

Identity is exact official five-digit `BuildingCode` plus school year. The [MSDS Section 25e guide](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/cepi/MSDS/Section25_User_Guide.pdf), dated March 9, 2023, defines building codes as five digits. Preserve native strings and raw IDs; names never establish identity. Public school entities must be distinguished from district/ISD aggregates and Unique Education Providers. Native `SchoolLevel` and complete historical grade evidence must exclude high-school grades from grade-school directories; unknown or protected grade evidence must not mean no high-school enrollment. Mixed-grade schools require an explicit separate scope.

The enrollment metadata explains that historical buildings can appear under a reorganized parent district without changing the underlying district aggregate. District metadata should be audited, but a district hierarchy/name change must not create an inferred identity. The [EEM definitions](https://www.michigan.gov/cepi/-/media/Project/Websites/cepi/EEM/EEM_Definitions.pdf?hash=C9A1518F88020B92EE88756A34845710&rev=ce80a972872044ac8e1731b754d12a9b) preserve school codes when administrative schools change physical location.

## Historical documents versus current revisions

The dated October 2024 memorandum, FY2024–25 FAQ and August 2024 direct-certification FAQ were downloaded and locally verified as contemporaneous documents. In contrast, the dynamically generated [2024–25 SNE rules](https://cepi.state.mi.us/msdsxml/generatebrr.aspx?component=376&format=pdf&name=SNE_2024-2025BR&schoolyear=18) retain Fall 2024/Spring 2025/EOY 2025 collection names but are revised May 13, 2026. They define current-school-year eligibility codes 1 (Free), 2 (Reduced) and 5 (Free direct-certification extension). The [2024–25 School Demographics rules](https://cepi.state.mi.us/msdsxml/generatebrr.aspx?component=350&format=pdf&name=SchoolDemographics_2024-2025BR&schoolyear=18) similarly retain those historical collections but were revised September 21, 2026. They specify five-character numeric text allowing leading zeros for school/facility IDs. These are current revisions of historical collection rules, not archived 2024 publications.

Old search results and revision parameters for the M-STEP/PSAT interpretive guides did not pin historical content: the downloaded M-STEP guide is Spring 2026, revised August 2026, and the PSAT guide is Spring 2026, revised September 2026. The dated [August 21, 2025 Spotlight](https://www.michigan.gov/mde/-/media/Project/Websites/mde/OEAA/Spotlight-Newsletter/2025-08-21-Spotlight.pdf?rev=2882c1b557c842c69c58f33dcf68bfcc&hash=B80B0EAC010865CD39931DE8AA67DDD0), page 7, and [September 25, 2025 Spotlight](https://www.michigan.gov/mde/-/media/Project/Websites/mde/OEAA/Spotlight-Newsletter/2025-09-25-Spotlight.pdf?rev=db5f115ef969420ea5a10be2eb63fdda&hash=3BC8E102635BE9C86EB66AB443800AC1), page 5, establish the historical guides' release but do not preserve their contents. The downloaded 2026 guides must not be presented as 2025 checksummed evidence.

## Adapter coverage and rebuild

`scripts/prepare_michigan.py` writes two independent canonical datasets, `mi-mstep-2025` and `mi-psat8-2025`, from `data/source/michigan.json`. The committed extract preserves selected native assessment rows with original record numbers, all 3,293 public school enrollment profiles, excluded profiles, protected cells, and the native-file and primary-document checksums. Delivery addresses and temporary URLs are absent. Default rebuilds require no network or raw downloads.

The historical enrollment file identifies 2,114 pure grade-school profiles after excluding mixed/high types, any nonzero or protected grade 9–12 or ungraded enrollment, and unknown lower-grade configurations. Nineteen of these lack exact individual income. The remaining 2,095 include primary schools without enrolled tested grades; they are not automatically model members. Each subject requires every explicit enrolled assessment grade, including a potentially enrolled grade represented by `<10`. A legitimate noncontiguous 3, 6, 7 configuration stays explicit; a minimum-to-maximum range must not invent grades 4 and 5.

| Separate comparison | School profiles | Math model | ELA model | Combined model |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| M-STEP grades 3–7 | 1,954 | 1,532 | 1,662 | 1,520 |
| PSAT grade 8 | 677 | 492 | 593 | 476 |

All 677 PSAT profiles also appear in the M-STEP directory under their exact native building IDs, with separate assessment observations and regressions. A profile with unavailable results remains searchable and retains its exclusions. M-STEP school rates are `100 × sum(TotalMet) / sum(NumberAssessed)` over the complete required grade set; PSAT uses the separate grade 8 counts. School grade mixes can differ, and the M-STEP model does not adjust for grade composition.

M-STEP Math excludes 289 profiles for unavailable counts, 120 for protected response percentages, and 13 for missing grades; ELA excludes 178, 101, and 13 respectively. PSAT Math excludes 129, 53, and 3; ELA excludes 42, 38, and 4. These categories use the adapter's first-failure priority and are school/subject counts rather than individual masked-row counts. Source counts, top-two aggregates and all visible performance bins reconcile; assessment/enrollment building, district, ISD, entity and level metadata agree. No name-based identity or inferred suppression value is used.

All six models use externally studentized residuals and have verified counts for every eligible member, so approximate conditional sampling intervals are available. Combined is the equal Math/ELA mean. PSAT's grade 8 benchmark percentages are separate from grade-specific M-STEP achievement thresholds. This snapshot adds neither Michigan high-school results nor prior-year history, MI-Access, mixed-grade models or a national achievement scale.

To refresh the extract, use the official public bulk form with school year 2024–25, all ISDs/districts/schools, and the two selected file names. Save the delivered authentic CSVs under the native filenames above and retain the primary documents listed in the adapter. `--extract` checks their full checksums before reading them; a revised file requires a fresh audit and explicit source-contract update rather than silent reuse.

```sh
.venv/bin/python scripts/prepare_michigan.py --extract  # original pinned raw sources present
.venv/bin/python scripts/prepare_michigan.py            # offline from the committed extract
.venv/bin/python scripts/export_catalog.py
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_michigan.py' -v
```

The targeted tests independently refit deleted-school regressions, check all six model populations and interval availability, exercise protected responses/counts and incomplete grades, reject wrong-year/identity/assessment/metadata mutations, and import both namespaces twice while preserving unrelated data. The final integration ran both canonical imports repeatedly, preserved all nine pre-existing logical tables and all 172 other served JSON files, retained Utah’s audit hold, and had no foreign-key violations. All 260 Python tests and 12 URL-state tests passed. Browser verification covered both selectors, first searches after reload, the 75-control list limit, explicit empty selection, selected schools outside the first list page, filters, model-population stability, URL restoration, interval charts and the annual table alternative. School map coordinates remain unavailable in this native extract.

Those raw flags remain intact; a diagnostic flag change is not used to approve a school cohort.
