# Ohio 2024–25 source audit: income population remains unresolved

Ohio is held from modeling. This audit inspected actual 2024–25 files and the dated FY25 EMIS manual; it does not use the later FY26 manual as evidence that FY25 is unavailable. Exact school proficiency is discoverable, but neither the broad public economic-disadvantage percentage nor the narrower nutrition-program candidate has yet established an individual, school-level income population for the full reporting period.

The [committed audit](../data/source/ohio-audit.json) records primary URLs, SHA-256 checksums, workbook schemas, the 15 unapproved grade-school candidates, and every other nutrition row with a first exclusion reason. There is no Ohio dataset descriptor, import, browser export or ready-state claim.

## Native 2024–25 downloads and identity

The official [Report Card download page](https://reportcard.education.ohio.gov/home/download) exposes a public categories API in its served application. The current-year categories response also lists historical 2024–25 files. Follow the observed `fileLocation` values; a later current-year selector does not change the school year in the named workbook. Public download authorization from the served application is not retained in committed provenance.

The audited files are [Building Details](https://eduprdreportcardstorage1.blob.core.windows.net/data-download-2025/2025_Building_Details.xlsx), [Building Enrollment](https://eduprdreportcardstorage1.blob.core.windows.net/data-download-2025/24-25_Enrollment_Building.xlsx), [Building Achievement](https://eduprdreportcardstorage1.blob.core.windows.net/data-download-2025/24-25_Achievement_Building.xlsx), and [Building Economic Disadvantage](https://eduprdreportcardstorage1.blob.core.windows.net/data-download-2025/BUILDING_ECON_DIS_2425.xlsx). The bare blob links may require the public download page's current authorization. The extracts retain the bare authoritative locations and public discovery page.

`Building_Details` reports six-digit building and district IRNs, student group, Enrollment and Enrollment Percent. Its `Economic Disadvantage` group supplies a published percentage with one decimal of precision, separate from `All Students` enrollment. The disaggregated economic workbook supplies only economically disadvantaged versus not disadvantaged; it does not separate individually verified eligibility from the unverified CEP codes. Neither workbook supports reconstructing an exact income count from a rounded percentage. The independent [FY2025 District Profile definition](https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/Finance-and-Funding/School-Payment-Reports/District-Profile-Reports/FY2025-District-Profile-Report) identifies the 2025 Report Card economic percentage as a fraction of total student population, while warning that enrollment definitions depend on context. That profile excludes community schools and does not independently establish their income timing rule.

The same-year [CCD directory](https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/Data/zip/ccd_sch_029_2425_w_1a_073025.zip) supplies `OH-districtIRN-schoolIRN` state identities and full NCES school IDs. The narrow discovery requires exact six-digit native IRNs, agreement with this state identity, operational directory status, one operational school in the LEA, and complete unadjusted grade offers and reconciled [2024–25 membership](https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/Data/zip/ccd_sch_052_2425_l_1a_073025.zip). School names establish no identity. Nutrition site IDs have the published eight-digit `00` prefix; stripping exactly that prefix can preserve the native six-digit namespace. It does not prove that a nutrition reporting site serves only that school.

## Dated FY25 economic-disadvantage definition

The [FY25 EMIS manual, August 21, 2024 compilation](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Data/EMIS/EMIS-Documentation/Current-EMIS-Manual/FY25-EMIS-Manual_Combined_08-21-24.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US), section 2.5, printed pages 6–7 (PDF pages 158–159), defines `FD110` as a student attribute with effective start/end dates. The section was revised August 15, 2024. Individual economic qualification can arise through approved free/reduced eligibility, an eligible household member, qualifying public assistance, or an income-qualified Title I form. Codes 1/3 identify economic qualification outside the CEP-specific codes; 6/7 identify verified qualification in CEP; 4/5 identify students in CEP whose qualification has not been established.

The same dated section permits Provision 2, Provision 3 and CEP buildings or districts to code all pupils economically disadvantaged even when some are not individually income eligible. A student may have multiple FD110 values within a school year. Consequently, the broad published ED percentage cannot be approved merely because it is numeric, at most 100%, or close to nutrition eligibility counts. A public aggregate isolating the individual codes, or an affirmative complete operating-program and income-population audit, is needed.

The currently served [Student Enrollment technical document](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Data/Report-Card-Resources/Traditional-Report-Cards/Student-Enrollment.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US) is now labeled 2025–26 and describes annual FTE. Its revision history refers to FY25, but a later overwritten guide is not independent proof of FY25's exact school-level income time/status rule. This audit does not describe the native 2025 income percentage as an October headcount or substitute the CCD fall denominator.

## Nutrition evidence and remaining scope gates

The official [October 2024 FY2025 nutrition workbook](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Student-Supports/Food-and-Nutrition/Resources-and-Tools-for-Food-and-Nutrition/Data-for-Free-and-Reduced-Price-Meal-Eligibility/October-2024-FY2025-Data-for-Free-and-Reduced-Price-Meal-Eligibilty.xlsx.aspx?lang=en-US) contains 3,559 identified sites: 1,872 Traditional, 1,638 CEP and 49 Provision 2; 18 further sheet rows have no site identity. Traditional is an affirmative source value, rather than an inference from absence in a CEP list. The [dated FY25 nutrition definitions](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Student-Supports/Food-and-Nutrition/Resources-and-Tools-for-Food-and-Nutrition/Data-for-Free-and-Reduced-Price-Meal-Eligibility/Description-for-Data-for-Free-and-Reduced-Price-Meal-Eligibility-2024-2025.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US), pages 1–3, distinguish individual applications, Provision 2 base-year counts, CEP claiming percentages and meal-access enrollment. Traditional enrollment is the highest daily October population with meal access; CEP enrollment refers to April. These are not automatically the report-card income denominator.

The definitions explicitly say that the Office of Nutrition keeps no record of the individual schools within a combined reporting site. Sponsors with no October claim are also absent. The source field identifies each site's provision type, but the inspected schema does not independently resolve every annual Provision 3/status circumstance needed to exclude the FY25 EMIS blanket-coding permission. None of these nutrition counts is approved for a school model, and no count-versus-enrollment comparison repairs reporting-site scope.

The [FY25 CEP notification](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Student-Supports/Food-and-Nutrition/Resources-and-Tools-for-Food-and-Nutrition/Community-Eligibility-Option/Community-Eligibility-Provision-Annual-Notification-for-School-Year-2024-2025-Site.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US) was created May 1, 2024. Eligible or near-eligible status does not establish participation, and its pre-year currently-participating indicator is not an exhaustive FY25 operating roster. The [dated FY25 CEP agreement](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Student-Supports/Food-and-Nutrition/Resources-and-Tools-for-Food-and-Nutrition/Community-Eligibility-Option/CEP_SA_Participation_Form_2024-2025-Fill-and-Sign.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US), pages 2–3, establishes the annual July 1, 2024–June 30, 2028 framework and June 30 election/withdrawal deadlines. It does not supply the candidates' operating histories or missing reporting-site mapping.

## Proficiency and unapproved narrow discovery

[EDC v3.1 Ohio 2025](https://www.eddatacenter.org/api/data/3.1?state=OH&year=2025) has exact school `All Students`, `Regular`, `G38`, Math/ELA percentages for Ohio's State Tests, levels 3–5. Its [codebook](https://www.eddatacenter.org/data_codebooks/EDC_codebook_v3.1.xlsx) describes G38 as the SEA-sourced grades 3–8 aggregate. The [technical documentation](https://www.eddatacenter.org/data_documentation/EDC_technical_documentation_v3.1.pdf) describes that grade population and Ohio's primary data-request provenance. This is documented aggregate provenance, not an independent recapture of each native Ohio source total. Source valid-score business rules have not been independently verified. If an income route passes, use only exact aggregate percentages and leave tested counts and all sampling interval endpoints null. Never average native grade indicators without verified valid-score weights. The all-subject Performance Index and EDC's constructed G38 average scale score do not supply Math/ELA proficiency.

Selecting affirmative Traditional sites whose sponsor/site/building/district IRNs agree, then requiring one operational CCD school per LEA and verified pure grade-school enrollment, yields 15 profiles before looking at outcomes. Eight have exact paired G38 percentages; seven have no such pair. All are public community schools. No candidate is approved. Even if the missing scope evidence is obtained, this would be a small restricted community-school cohort, not statewide Ohio coverage or a comparison with excluded traditional-district schools.

Independent numerical diagnostics on the eight prospective pairs find a rank-two income/intercept design, maximum leverage 0.4864, positive deleted-regression variances and finite externally studentized residuals for Math, ELA and equal-weight Combined. This confirms numerical feasibility only. It does not resolve income eligibility, operating-program scope, assessment denominators or selection bias. No counts, intervals, outcome-based population pruning or publication follow from this diagnostic.

The existing CCD DC route also remains held: the [Final 2a SEA notes](https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/xls/SY_2024-25_CCD_Final_2a_Data_Notes.xlsx) identify LEAs allocating school lunch/DC counts to one building. Named count-exceeds-membership exceptions are not an exhaustive affected-LEA registry. A remaining count below enrollment cannot prove individual-school membership. See the [national DC guide](ccd-state-data.md).
