# Assessment guide

The separate [tests and standards page](../assessments.html) begins [GitHub issue #1](https://github.com/robot-assisted-projects/FRPL-RMA/issues/1). It explains the latest assessment data included in each ready comparison, rather than claiming to describe newer statewide testing contracts.

`scripts/export_assessment_guide.py` reads the committed manifest and 50-state source ledger. Only ready region datasets are eligible. The latest year is chosen separately for each dataset and comparison level; New York City's 2026 NYSTP and the state's 2025 snapshot remain separate, as do older high-school series. Assessment labels, tested grades, standards and source URLs are retained exactly. Each row links only the ready regions whose served model metadata matches that definition. Utah's unready definitions are excluded. States without a release remain visible with their source hold.

The map groups directly documented assessment-family names. Different grade cohorts, alternate assessments, state proficiency thresholds and regional model populations are still shown separately in the list. A shared color is not evidence of interchangeable scores or equal target ambition. Multiple families in the chosen population receive stripes. The state filter and list provide an accessible alternative to the geographic map; map states also support keyboard selection.

Provider information is a separate evidence gate. Michigan's grade 8 PSAT owner is documented as College Board in the existing native audit, with its source URL and checksum retained. Nine released 2025 Smarter Balanced/SBAC definitions in California, Delaware, Nevada and Washington identify the **Smarter Balanced Assessment Consortium as assessment developer**, including explicit Los Angeles and Clark County bindings to their verified native assessment systems, grades and year. Four separate SAT definitions now identify **College Board as assessment owner**: Illinois statewide and Chicago for 2024, and Colorado and Delaware for 2025. The 2025 role explicitly retains the source's **with licensors** qualification. District and statewide model populations remain separate. Consortium ownership/delivery roles and SAT developer/delivery roles remain unaudited. Other provider fields remain null until primary evidence establishes the applicable-year role. A familiar assessment name, consortium membership or download domain does not establish a delivery contractor or approve another test family.

After LAUSD, Miami-Dade, Clark County, Broward, Hillsborough, Orange, Palm Beach, Gwinnett, Fairfax, Houston, Wake and Montgomery County integration, the regenerated guide contains 77 latest released definitions across 39 states. Each district has its own region link and model population. California retains separate statewide and LAUSD grade/high-school definitions; Florida, Nevada, Georgia, Virginia, Texas, North Carolina and Maryland retain separate statewide and district grade-school definitions. Miami-Dade, Broward, Hillsborough, Orange, Palm Beach, Gwinnett, Fairfax, Houston, Wake and Montgomery provider roles remain unverified. Each district's native assessment definition links only its independently fitted population; it does not inherit a developer or contractor from another region. Montgomery preserves native regular MCAP All Grades Levels 3 and 4, accelerated mathematics courses and the applicable waiver in its separate district population. Wake retains the exact regular EOG/grade-8 Math 1 family and GLP Level 3 and above, with its own district population and link. Gwinnett preserves Georgia's name, year, grades, Levels 3–4 standard and source, with only the population wording changed from separate state model to separate district model. Clark County has an explicit eight-field definition binding for the consortium developer role, including exact standard and source URL; later definition drift or a new year requires another audit. The 11 source holds and unaudited provider/target fields remain explicit.

The [2024–25 consortium technical report's system overview](https://technicalreports.smarterbalanced.org/2024-25_interim-report/_book/index.html) credits the consortium with creating the assessment system, explicitly including summative assessments, and describes its role in maintaining that system. Its [administration chapter](https://technicalreports.smarterbalanced.org/2024-25_interim-report/_book/test-admin.html) distinguishes consortium services from services members must secure. The official [2024–25 innovation awards program](https://smarterbalanced.org/teaching-and-learning-innovation-awards/) independently lists the four jurisdictions as members during its November 2024–February 2025 application period. These are narrow developer-role findings; interim reliability and validity findings are not transferred to summative comparisons. The reviewed extract in `data/source/assessment-provider-evidence.json` retains source URLs, raw-file checksums, source sections and exact definition bindings. The exporter verifies its pinned checksum offline, rejects a changed bound definition and deduplicates provenance by source ID so the separate College Board evidence remains present. Future years require a new audit.

The [ISBE-hosted Spring 2024 SAT student guide](https://www.isbe.net/Documents/2024-Student-Guide-SAT.pdf), Section 8(a), printed page 35 / physical page 38, identifies College Board's ownership of digital SAT test content. Its archived 2024 context applies to the separately bound Illinois statewide and Chicago SAT definitions, including that year's SAT with Essay administration. The [archived Spring 2025 SAT School Day rules](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/sat-sd-testing-rules-archived-spring2025.pdf), physical pages 1 and 9, apply only to Spring 2025 and expressly include licensors in the ownership statement. The [Colorado Spring 2025 student guide](https://www.cde.state.co.us/assessment/sat-student-guide) and [Delaware 2025 assessment summary](https://news.delaware.gov/files/2025/08/assessment_summary_25.pdf) corroborate the two native grade-11 assessment populations. Original state records independently establish the served year and test. A currently overwritten technical-manual URL serving a 2026 revision is excluded from historical role evidence.

Each SAT binding checks all eight native definition fields: ID, state, name, year, level, grades, standard and source URL. No benchmark, grade span, population, family label, region link or regression changes. Michigan retains its independently sourced PSAT role and has no released SAT definition. Ownership does not establish a developer or a state delivery contractor, and it supplies no common proficiency threshold. Original source bytes, sections and short excerpts remain auditable through the pinned provider extract; ordinary guide export stays offline.

Relative target ambition remains unaudited; the page assigns no `+` or `−` indicators. Those markers require evidence comparing equivalent instruments, grades, subjects, years and cut scores. Identifying a shared developer does not make the state thresholds or regression populations interchangeable.

State boundaries derive from the [2024 Census cartographic archive](https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/GENZ2024/shp/cb_2024_us_state_500k.zip), already used by the state location audits. Its SHA-256 is `3e81cbb6cf5f60d9b01ef3e730afa14d61ce5f784252a0b33a696135e71447bc`. The exporter checks all 50 native FIPS/postal/name identities, excludes DC and territories, retains exterior polygon rings and islands, rejects unexpected holes in this pinned source, and simplifies coordinates with ring-area protection. D3's Albers USA projection provides Alaska and Hawaii insets. Boundary provenance and simplification metadata are committed with `data/us-states.geojson`.

Remaining issue #1 work is provider evidence and defensible equivalent-test threshold comparisons.
